## 3. Information we may process

Depending on the features you use, LIBRIS may process:

- **Account information:** email address, profile name, authentication identifiers, and account settings.
- **Sign-in information:** information returned by a sign-in provider, such as Google, limited to the permissions and fields actually requested.
- **Library and reading information:** saved books, shelves, ratings, reading status, reading position, reading sessions, bookmarks, highlights, and notes.
- **Search and recommendation information:** search terms, selected genres or moods, recommendation feedback, and preferences you choose to provide.
- **Assistant information:** prompts, conversation history, and only the book passages or reading context needed for an assistant feature you choose to use.
- **Uploaded content:** EPUB/PDF files and associated metadata, only if personal uploads are enabled.
- **Technical and security information:** basic request, error, device, or log information that the deployed infrastructure actually collects.
- **Support communications:** information you send when asking for help or reporting a problem.

We aim to collect only information reasonably needed for a clearly stated purpose. Do not add sensitive or optional fields without a defined need and appropriate safeguards.

## 4. Why we use information

Where a feature is active and a valid legal basis applies, information may be used to:

- create and secure accounts;
- provide search, book discovery, library, and reading features;
- save progress, notes, and preferences;
- generate requested recommendations or assistant answers;
- operate, debug, and protect the service;
- respond to support requests and user reports;
- comply with legal obligations; and
- understand service reliability and improve features using appropriately limited analytics, if analytics are enabled.

The applicable legal basis depends on the purpose, the actual processing, and the law that applies. Do not state that every activity is based on consent or legitimate interests without assessing each activity.

## 5. AI features and external processing

If LIBRIS offers AI-powered recommendations or a Reading Companion, the feature should explain what information will be sent for processing before or when the user invokes it. Depending on the implementation, this may include a prompt, book metadata, a passage, chapter context, or reading position.

If an external AI provider processes this information, identify that provider in the live vendor disclosure and explain the provider's role, relevant retention settings, and any applicable international transfers.
