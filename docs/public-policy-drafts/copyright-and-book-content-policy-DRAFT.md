## 1. Respect for authors and publishers

LIBRIS aims to help users discover and organise books while respecting the rights of authors, publishers, libraries, and other rights holders. Book metadata, cover art, excerpts, and full text can each have different rights and licences.

A catalogue entry or external link does not mean LIBRIS owns a book or has permission to host or redistribute its full text.

## 2. How book access should be labelled

The product should distinguish clearly between:
- public-domain full text;
- openly licensed or open-access text;
- authorised library borrowing;
- a limited preview or excerpt;
- a retailer or publisher purchase link;
- an audiobook or other external service; and
- metadata-only catalogue records.

Show the provider and relevant access terms where practical. Do not label a book “free” or “available” unless the source supports that claim and it is current enough for the use.

## 3. Personal uploads

If uploads are enabled, users must have the rights or permission necessary to upload and use the file. Uploaded files should be private by default, limited to supported formats and configured quotas, and accessible only to the account owner and authorised systems needed to provide the feature.

Do not make uploaded books public or use them for unrelated model training. Any processing by an external AI provider must be disclosed and technically limited to the permitted purpose.

## 4. Rights complaints

Before allowing public uploads, reviews containing substantial excerpts, or community sharing, publish a working rights-reporting process. It should collect the claimant's contact details, identify the work and disputed material, explain the basis of the claim, and provide a process for review and appropriate action. Do not invent a formal legal takedown process without legal review.

## 5. Product controls

- Verify provider terms before importing or caching metadata, covers, or full text.
- Store source, licence/access type, source URL, and last-checked date where available.
- Respect rate limits and attribution requirements.
- Keep metadata access separate from permission to process or host full text.
- Avoid unauthorised scraping or redistribution.
- Provide a way to report incorrect metadata or broken links.
- Review provider terms before sending copyrighted passages to external AI services.
